Edge Wear: Paint Chipping to Bare Metal

A classic game-art technique: edge wear — flat surfaces keep their paint, while edges/corners show bare metal from repeated impact. The trick: Fresnel measures 'how close to an edge this is', a hard-edged Color Ramp (Constant interpolation) turns that into an all-or-nothing mask, and the mask drives a Mix Shader to swap between two completely different materials.

1. Step 1: Prepare a Painted Material and a Bare-Metal Material

The existing Principled BSDF is the paint: Metallic 0, Roughness around 0.4, a normal painted look.

Drag in a new Principled BSDF for bare metal: light gray Base Color, Metallic set to 1, lower Roughness (e.g. 0.2). Don't wire it up yet.

2. Step 2: Wire Both Together with Mix Shader

Add a Mix Shader (Shader category), connect the painted and bare-metal Principled nodes to its two Shader inputs, then wire it to Material Output's Surface, replacing the original direct connection.

3. Step 3: Build a Hard-Edge Mask with Fresnel + Color Ramp

Add a Fresnel node (Input category), raising IOR a bit (e.g. 2.2) to concentrate the edge zone.

Add a Color Ramp, switch Interpolation to Constant, and set stops: position 0 black, position 0.6 white — no transition zone at all, only pure black (fully painted) or pure white (fully bare) with a razor-sharp wear boundary instead of a soft blend.

4. Step 4: Drive the Blend with the Mask

Connect Color Ramp's Color output to Mix Shader's Fac, replacing the fixed slider.

The front-facing area should stay fully painted, with only the grazing edges suddenly flipping to bare metal — that's edge wear: a hard, decisive switch rather than a soft gradient like typical dirt masks.
