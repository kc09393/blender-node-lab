Subsurface Scattering

Simulates light entering beneath the surface and scattering back out — key for skin, wax, milk.

Try it in the sandbox 繁體中文

Beginner explanation

Without subsurface scattering, skin looks plasticky. This node lets edges (like a backlit ear or nose) glow faintly red.

Technical notes

Real SSS needs multi-sample blurring beneath the surface (screen-space or a Burley diffusion profile) — a full ray-tracing/post-process job. This sandbox uses a simplified approximation instead: a Fresnel term drives an edge-tinted glow using the Radius vector's R/G/B ratios (the default has red traveling further than blue, which is why backlit ears/noses glow reddish), scaled by Scale. It isn't true subsurface diffusion, but the direction and look are right.
